Frontline Commander
Launch Outlook & Franchise Plan

One battlefield, two audiences: built to stream, deep enough to main. The plan for taking it to market — and the two games that follow it.

ANNEX A

The Product at v1.0

Frontline Commander is a single-file, zero-install lane-battler in the Age-of-War lineage, finished to launch depth. It is not a shell over a demo loop: under the frontend sits a genuine systems game — a damage-type × armour-class counter web across 14 units, per-lane terrain, weather, morale & suppression, EW, veterancy, an in-battle tech tree, five-age Evolution, a persistent conquest map with reserve-CP logistics, and a career backend that remembers every battle you fight and every general you fight it against.

14units in the counter web
7game modes
9doctrines
20+chat commands
7playable nations
0installs required

Everything spectacular is opt-out. Chaos mode, random events, gags, and chat sabotage each have their own switch, so the same build serves a Twitch mob one hour and a solo strategist the next. That toggle architecture is the product's core commercial bet.

ANNEX B

Positioning & Pillars

Category: streamable strategy. One-line pitch: an RTS where the chat is a player — and the enemy generals remember you.

Chat is a player

Anonymous Twitch IRC, no OAuth. Viewers surge lanes, summon bosses, drop supplies — and 50 chanting "nuke" glasses the field. Information-warfare commands gaslight the streamer's own screen. Clips manufacture themselves.

Real depth underneath

A measured counter web, terrain, weather, EW, veterancy and logistics. The balance gradient is playtested by machine: Recruit teaches, Legendary beats a strong scripted player two games in three.

A career that remembers

Service Record, per-unit history, medals, and rivalry memory — beat a general three times and their next intro admits it. Character depth players keep coming back to.

Session in five minutes

10-second prep phase, one HTML file, runs anywhere a browser runs. The distance from link to first kill is under a minute.

"You. Three times you've humiliated me. There is no plan B this time — there is only artillery." — Marshal Dubois, to a returning player. This is what retention sounds like.

ANNEX E

Revenue Model & Monetization

The core loop stays free forever — a paywalled counter web kills the clip economy Annex B is built on. Revenue sits around the free match: cosmetics, a career-facing pass, sponsor-safe chat commerce, and a premium sequel that monetizes the depth Grand Theater's engine already earns. No pay-to-win: every purchasable in Frontline Commander is cosmetic, convenience, or content — never a stat.

In-Game Commerce (Frontline Commander, live build)

Cosmetic IAP

National unit skins, HQ banners, victory-flyover variants, killcam filters. Sold individually ($1–3) or bundled in the Kit above. Never touches balance.

Narrator voice packs

The premium ElevenLabs narrator is a $2.99 unlock per voice, three voices at launch. Direct revenue line from a system already built — no new engineering, just a paywall in front of an existing feature.

Battle Pass

Seasonal track tied to matches played, not spend — doctrine cosmetics, chat-command sound stings, title-screen frames. Free track exists; paid track ($7.99) doubles reward pace.

Sponsor chat takeovers

A sponsor's brand skins the chat-power UI for a scheduled event window (e.g. a sponsored "nuke" reskin during a creator's stream). Sold to brands per-event, not per-player — keeps the free build free while monetizing the exact chat-integration surface Annex B leads with.

ANNEX F

The Franchise — Three Titles, One War

The Iron Marshal is the franchise voice; the rival generals are its recurring cast; the counter web is its shared combat grammar. Each title serves a different session length with the same fiction.

Now · v1.0 shipped

Frontline CommanderLive

The lane battler. Five-minute sessions, streamer-native, career memory. The franchise's front door and clip factory.

Title II · +12 months

Frontline Commander: Grand Theater

The staff-college game, productized from our existing full-scale war simulator engine — the validated one with sensor physics, belief & disinformation modeling, logistics tonnage, casualty chains and orbital warfare. Sessions are campaigns, not battles. Same generals, now across whole theaters; your Commander career imports as your officer dossier. Positioned against grand strategy, priced as the "serious" tier.

Title III · +20 months

Frontline: Pocket Wars

Mobile roguelite auto-battler: draft a doctrine, run a 10-node war map, one-hand play. The counter web becomes a deck; the generals become bosses with the same voices and grudges. Free with cosmetic nations — the reach title that feeds players back to the other two.

Why this order works: Commander builds the audience and the cast cheaply; Grand Theater monetizes the depth-seeking segment with an engine that already exists; Pocket Wars converts reach into franchise identity. Shared save signals — rank, rivalries, medals — make each title a funnel into the others.
