01 · The pitch

What it is, in one breath

Bank Command Points, slam units into three lanes, and crack the enemy HQ before they crack yours — while an announcer screams your kill streaks and the stream chat loses its mind. It's the Age of War lineage rebuilt as a modern, streamable roguelite-flavoured skirmish game.

The simulator it came from answers "what would really happen?" This game answers "was that awesome?" Every design decision downstream of that split is deliberate: matches are short, feedback is loud, the numbers are legible at a glance, and the whole thing is built so a streamer's viewers understand what just happened without being told. It borrows the simulator's concepts — combined-arms counters, doctrine identities, morale-of-the-crowd momentum — and throws out its fidelity wherever fidelity is slower than fun.

02 · Function inventory

Everything the game does

The complete system list, grouped the way a design doc would group it. Each card is a shipped, verified subsystem in the current build.

◆Core loop

A real-time three-lane tug-of-war. Command Points bank over time; you spend them to deploy units that auto-advance, auto-fight, and smash the enemy HQ. Win by dropping the enemy HQ to zero.

  • Command-Point economy with a hard cap and a visible bank meter
  • Three lanes; deploy by picking a card then clicking a lane (or Q/W/E quick-deploy)
  • Per-unit deploy cooldowns to stop single-card spam
  • Free missile strike on its own recharge — a skill-timing button, not a purchase

▲Units & combined-arms counters

Eight unit types that hard-counter each other, so composition is a real decision rather than a spam contest.

  • Rifleman, AT Team, Tank, IFV, Artillery, AA Vehicle, Attack Heli, Kamikaze Drone
  • Armour shrugs off small arms; AT and drones melt armour; AA erases aircraft; arty arcs over the front
  • Fliers ignore ground fire unless the enemy brought AA or their own air
  • Each carries HP, damage, range, speed, splash and a role — readable from its silhouette

✦Doctrines (perk loadouts)

Six doctrines lifted from the simulator's families, here reframed as build-defining perk sets you pick before the match.

  • Blitzkrieg (cheap fast armour) · Mass Assault (cheap infantry + economy)
  • Air Dominant · Precision Strike · Drone Swarm · Defense in Depth
  • Each rewrites unit cost, speed, damage, HQ health or strike cooldown
  • Unlocked by rank, so the roster opens up as you climb

⏱Modes & difficulty

Three shapes of match crossed with four AI tiers, so the same core loop covers a quick win, a score chase, and an endurance run.

  • Skirmish — the main event, destroy the HQ
  • Blitz — two-minute clock, most HQ damage wins
  • Survival — endless waves that harden every 30s; how long do you hold?
  • Recruit / Veteran / Elite / Legendary scale enemy economy and unit quality

✹Game feel & feedback ("juice")

The layer that turns a state machine into a spectacle. Everything that happens is felt, not just tallied.

  • Screenshake, explosion particles, muzzle flashes, floating damage, expanding shockwave rings
  • Combo meter with escalating announcer calls — Triple Kill → Rampage → Unstoppable → Legendary
  • Live killfeed, screen-flash on HQ hits, and per-unit HP bars
  • Procedural WebAudio SFX — no asset files, deploy blips, booms, a victory sting

📺Streamer mode

A first-class toggle, not an afterthought — the game changes shape to broadcast well.

  • Simulated hype chat that reacts to combos, waves, HQ hits and clip moments
  • A LIVE banner and a "CLIP THAT!" flash on big streaks
  • Bigger, cleaner readouts so a viewer follows the match without commentary
  • Everything runs off the same events the game already fires — zero extra state

★Progression & meta

A reason to come back between matches, all saved to the browser with no account.

  • XP and ranks that unlock doctrines on a curve
  • Seven medals (First Blood, Killstreak, Flawless, Last Stand, Legend, Blitz King, Grand Marshal)
  • Per-mode high scores and a career win/loss line
  • Per-match score from damage, kills and best combo, plus a mode-specific bonus

⌘Controls & tech

Approachable input over a lean, dependency-free engine.

  • Click-to-deploy, number keys to pick a card, Q/W/E to fire into a lane, Space to pause
  • Sound toggle, pause screen, abandon-with-confirm
  • Single HTML file, Canvas 2D, requestAnimationFrame loop, responsive to viewport
  • Runs fully offline; state persists in localStorage

Why keep them separate files

The simulator's value is that every number is honest; the game's value is that every second is fun. Those are opposing masters. Splitting them means each can be pushed to its own extreme without the other dragging on it — and the game inherits the simulator's ideas (counters, doctrines, momentum) for free while paying none of its complexity tax.

The dedicated Streamer Mode is the differentiator here. Most games leave "does this stream well?" to chance; this one treats the broadcast as a target platform, with an event bus already firing the hooks a real Twitch/YouTube integration would subscribe to. The simulated chat is a stand-in for that integration — and a genuinely funny bit on its own.

05 · Market outlook

Does it belong on Steam?

Short answer: the genre is proven, the niche is streamer-shaped, and the build is a real vertical slice — but it ships as a small premium title or free-to-play-with-cosmetics, not a AAA swing. Here's the read.

Positioning statement

"A two-minute war you can win on stream." The fast, funny, counter-driven lane-battler for people who want the feeling of commanding an army without a 40-hour grand-strategy manual — and for the streamers who want a game their chat can backseat-general in real time.

Target audience

Strategy-curious players bounced off by 4X/grand-strategy complexity; the auto-battler and roguelite-deckbuilder crowd; and — the wedge — small-to-mid streamers hunting a short-session, chat-interactive game that isn't yet saturated. Streamer mode is the marketing engine: every broadcast is an ad, and the chat-vote roadmap turns viewers into acquisition.

Path to a sellable v1.0

  1. Shipped · this buildDONE
    Vertical slice

    The full core loop, 3 modes, 6 doctrines, 8 units, juice, streamer mode, progression — playable and verified end-to-end with zero errors. Proves the fun is real.

  2. Phase 1 · content
    Depth & a campaign spine

    A short escalating campaign, 3–4 more units and 2–3 doctrines, per-doctrine unlock trees, and a daily-seed challenge for the leaderboard crowd. This is what turns a demo into a $7 purchase.

  3. Phase 2 · the streamer moat
    Real chat integration

    Wire the existing event bus to a Twitch/YouTube extension: chat votes on the enemy's next push, donates to a lane, triggers "boss" waves. The simulated chat becomes real chat. This is the differentiator no comp has nailed.

  4. Phase 3 · launch
    Steam page, demo, Next Fest

    Ship the free demo into a Steam Next Fest with a wishlist push and a streamer key drop. The genre's discoverability lives and dies on the demo — and this game's demo is its whole strength.

Honest risks

!Depth ceiling

Lane-pushers can feel solved fast. The counter system and doctrine builds buy real depth, but Phase 1 content is what keeps a $7 buyer past hour three.

!Discoverability

Steam is a red ocean for small strategy games. The streamer-native angle is the only realistic wedge — lean into it hard or don't ship.

!Art bar

The vector/HUD look is clean and readable but modest. It clips well; it may not thumbnail against art-heavy competitors without a signature visual hook.

06 · Verdict

The bottom line

This is a real game, not a tech demo — the core loop is fun in the first thirty seconds, the counters make composition a genuine decision, and the juice plus streamer mode give it a shape that broadcasts. As a small premium title (~$7) built around a chat-interactive streamer moat, it has a credible path on Steam. It is not a system-seller on its own — it needs Phase 1 content depth and Phase 2 real chat integration to convert wishlists into sales. But the hardest thing in game development — proving the core is fun — is already done, and it's sitting in a single 30 KB file that runs offline in any browser.
